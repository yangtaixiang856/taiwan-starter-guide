Emergency Contacts in Taiwan: Numbers and Phrases to Save

Save these numbers before you need them. The most important part is not memorizing every hotline. It is knowing who to call, what to say, and how to give your location.

Updated 2026-05-25 Official sources checked 2026-05-25 Mobile-friendly Students, expats, workers

Short answer

For immediate danger or police help, call 110. For fire, ambulance, or rescue, call 119. Save 165 for anti-fraud help, 113 for violence or protection support, 1955 for foreign worker labor help, and 1990 for daily-life questions for foreigners in Taiwan. Keep your Chinese address ready.

Who this guide is for

  • International students who need a small emergency card for dorm, campus, and late-night situations.
  • Expats and white-collar workers who want a clear Taiwan emergency contact list before moving.
  • Foreign workers who may need labor help, interpretation support, or safe referral channels.
  • Friends, roommates, schools, HR teams, and landlords helping a newcomer prepare for real life in Taiwan.

Save these Taiwan emergency numbers

Number Use it for What to prepare before calling
110 Police emergency, crime, theft, assault, traffic accident, suspicious event, urgent police help. Your location in Chinese, nearest landmark, what happened, whether anyone is injured, your phone number.
119 Fire, ambulance, rescue, urgent medical emergency. Chinese address, floor or room number, building entrance, symptoms or injury, nearby MRT station or convenience store.
165 Anti-fraud and scam consultation, suspicious calls, fake bank or delivery messages, online fraud concerns. Caller number, screenshots, bank transfer details, LINE or SMS messages, time and amount if money was sent.
113 Protection support for domestic violence, sexual assault, child or youth protection, stalking or threats. Safe location, whether you can speak safely, what happened, who is involved, whether you need police or shelter help.
1955 Foreign worker labor consultation, complaints, referral protection, interpretation, employer or broker issues. Employer name, broker name, workplace or dorm address, contract, payslip, photos, messages, and what help you need.
1990 Foreigner life consultation: visa, residence, work, education, tax, health insurance, transportation, personal safety, and daily life questions. Your question, location, status, school or employer name, and any official document or letter you are looking at.
1925 Mental health support hotline. Language support may be limited, so ask 1990, 1955, your school, or a trusted person for interpretation help if needed. Your location, whether you are safe now, whether you may hurt yourself or someone else, and a friend or office contact if possible.

If someone is in immediate danger: call 110 or 119 first. Do not wait for an email reply, social media comment, or website update.

Prepare a Chinese address card

Emergency dispatchers, taxi drivers, hospital staff, police, delivery staff, and nearby shop workers can help faster if you can show a clear address. Save this in your phone notes and screenshot it.

  • Chinese address: include city, district, road, lane, alley, number, floor, and room.
  • English address: useful for your own notes, embassy or representative office, and foreign friends.
  • Nearest landmark: MRT station, bus stop, convenience store, school gate, company building, hospital, or police station.
  • Building details: entrance code, elevator, floor, room number, guard desk, dorm name, or apartment name.
  • Your basic identity: name, nationality, passport or ARC number if safe to store, school or employer, emergency contact.

Simple phrases to say or show

English Chinese When to use it
I need the police. 我需要警察。 Crime, threat, assault, theft, accident, or urgent police help.
Please call an ambulance. 請幫我叫救護車。 Medical emergency, serious injury, allergic reaction, fainting, breathing trouble.
There is a fire. 這裡失火了。 Fire, smoke, gas smell, rescue situation.
I do not speak Chinese. Can you find someone who speaks English? 我不會說中文，可以找會英文的人嗎？ Police station, clinic, hospital, school office, HR, convenience store.
My address is here. 我的地址在這裡。 Show your address card or map pin.
I am a foreign student / foreign worker. 我是外國學生 / 外籍工作者。 Explaining why you may need school, HR, or interpretation help.

What to do in common situations

  1. Medical emergency: call 119, show your Chinese address, say what happened, and ask a roommate, guard, school office, or HR contact to help translate if possible.
  2. Theft or assault: call 110 or go to the nearest police station. Save photos, messages, receipts, and location details. Ask for a police report if you need it for insurance, passport replacement, or school or employer records.
  3. Scam or suspicious bank transfer: call 165 and contact your bank immediately. Keep screenshots, phone numbers, transfer records, and chat history.
  4. Domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking, or threats: call 113 if you can do so safely. If there is immediate danger, call 110 first.
  5. Workplace, broker, dorm, salary, or contract problem for a foreign worker: call 1955. Keep contract photos, payslips, broker messages, dorm address, and employer details.
  6. Visa, ARC, residence, health insurance, tax, or daily-life confusion: call 1990 or check the official agency. If it is urgent and involves safety, call 110 or 119 first.
  7. Lost passport: report to police if needed, contact your home country's representative office, and keep copies of your passport, ARC, entry stamp, and police report.

Realistic examples

Student with an allergic reaction: A student in a dorm feels her throat swelling after dinner. Her roommate calls 119, shows the dorm's Chinese address, says "請幫我叫救護車", and messages the dorm manager. Because the address and allergy note were saved in advance, the dispatcher and ambulance staff get clearer information.
Worker with a salary and dorm problem: A foreign worker is told to move dorms with no clear explanation and notices missing pay. He is not in immediate physical danger, so he calls 1955, prepares his contract, payslips, dorm address, broker messages, and employer name, then asks what official steps are available.

Common mistakes

  • Only saving numbers, not your address. A hotline number is less useful if you cannot explain where you are.
  • Waiting for a friend to reply during an emergency. If there is danger, call 110 or 119 first.
  • Using social media as the first source for official help. Groups are useful for support, but police, ambulance, labor, immigration, and protection hotlines are the official route.
  • Deleting evidence too quickly. Keep screenshots, receipts, transfer records, messages, photos, and call times.
  • Assuming every hotline has full English support at every moment. Ask for English, ask 1990 or 1955 for language support when appropriate, and keep a trusted translator contact if possible.

What to do next

  1. Save 110, 119, 165, 113, 1955, and 1990 in your phone.
  2. Create a note called "Taiwan emergency card" with your Chinese address and nearest landmark.
  3. Send the address card to your roommate, school international office, employer HR, and one trusted friend.
  4. If you have a health condition, save the English and Chinese names of the condition and medication if you know them.
  5. Read the related guides below so you know which office owns which problem.
