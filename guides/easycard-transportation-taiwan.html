How do EasyCard and transportation work in Taiwan?

Taiwan gets easier once you understand the layers: EasyCard for daily trips, MRT and buses for cities, TRA for regular rail, and HSR for fast west-coast travel.

Updated 2026-05-22 Transport sources checked 2026-05-22 Newcomers and students First-week setup

Short answer

Buy an EasyCard or iPASS after arrival. Add value at an MRT station or convenience store. Use it for Taipei Metro, many city buses, YouBike registration, and small everyday payments. For longer intercity trips, check whether you need a separate Taiwan Railway or High Speed Rail ticket.

Who this guide is for

  • New students and workers trying to move around Taipei, New Taipei, or other cities.
  • Newcomers who do not want to buy a scooter immediately.
  • People arriving at Taoyuan Airport and going to Taipei, New Taipei, or a university dorm.
  • Anyone confused by MRT, bus, TRA, HSR, YouBike, EasyCard, and iPASS.

Step-by-step: your first week transport setup

  1. Buy a stored-value card. EasyCard is the most common in Taipei and New Taipei. iPASS also works in many transport systems. You can usually buy cards at MRT stations and convenience stores.
  2. Add value before you need it. Top up at MRT machines, station counters, or many convenience stores. Keep enough balance for your next ride and one emergency transfer.
  3. Use MRT for predictable routes. Tap in at the gate, take the train, and tap out. Check first and last train times if you return late.
  4. Use buses for areas away from MRT. In Taipei and New Taipei, get used to tapping when boarding and leaving if the bus system asks for it. Watch the route direction carefully.
  5. Register YouBike only after you have the right setup. YouBike may require registration, a phone number, and a linked card or payment method. Always check the bike is returned correctly.
  6. Separate local transit from intercity trips. Taiwan Railway and Taiwan High Speed Rail are not the same system. For reserved seats or long trips, use official ticketing channels.
  7. Save your home address in Chinese. If you need a taxi late at night or after moving day, showing the Chinese address helps more than trying to pronounce it.

Airport arrival: getting into the city

If you land at Taoyuan Airport, common routes into Taipei or New Taipei include Airport MRT, bus, taxi, or airport pickup. Airport MRT is clear and usually easy if your luggage is manageable. A taxi can be worth it late at night, in heavy rain, or if your dorm or apartment is far from a station.

Realistic example: A newcomer lands at Taoyuan Airport with two suitcases. She buys mobile data first, takes Airport MRT to Taipei Main Station, then uses a taxi for the final 12-minute ride to a dorm because carrying luggage through one more transfer would be stressful.

How to think about each transport type

  • MRT: best for predictable city travel. Good signage, station maps, and easier navigation for newcomers.
  • Bus: useful for neighborhoods away from MRT. Check direction, stop name, and whether you need to tap when leaving.
  • YouBike: helpful for short local trips, but register properly and check the return light or app status after docking.
  • TRA: regular rail for many cities and local or regional trips. Some stored-value card use is possible, but rules and train types matter.
  • HSR: fastest for west-coast trips such as Taipei to Taichung, Tainan, or Zuoying. Usually plan it separately from local EasyCard travel.
  • Taxi and ride-hailing: useful at night, in rain, with luggage, or when public transport is inconvenient. Save the destination in Chinese.

Common mistakes

  • Confusing TRA and HSR. They use different stations in some cities, different ticketing, and different prices.
  • Not checking last trains. MRT is convenient, but not 24 hours. Late-night taxis may be needed.
  • Forgetting to tap correctly. On systems that require entry and exit records, missing a tap can create fare problems.
  • Assuming every store accepts EasyCard. Many places do, but keep a small amount of cash and another payment method.
  • Leaving a YouBike half-returned. Always make sure the bike is locked and the rental has ended.
  • Choosing housing without testing commute. Visit the route during the real commute time before signing a lease.

What to do next

  1. Buy an EasyCard or iPASS and add enough value for the first few days.
  2. Save your home, school, workplace, and nearest hospital addresses in Chinese.
  3. Install the official MRT, bus, or YouBike apps you actually need in your city.
  4. Test your school or work commute before choosing a long-term rental.
  5. For weekend trips, compare TRA and HSR using official fare and timetable pages.
