Prepare Before Moving to Taiwan: A First 30 Days Checklist

A simple, practical checklist for what to prepare before your flight, what to do during your first 7 days, and what to finish in your first month.

Last updated 2026-05-27 Students, expats, workers Airport arrival friendly Official-source checklist

Short answer

Before you arrive, prepare your documents, arrival address in Chinese, temporary mobile data, first-month money, and a clear plan for housing, transport, school or HR contact, and official mail. In your first week, solve phone, EasyCard, housing address, school or employer check-in, and bank route. In your first month, confirm ARC, NHI, banking, tax records, and any work or study rules that apply to your status.

Who this guide is for

  • International students preparing for university, exchange, Mandarin study, or a degree program in Taiwan.
  • White-collar workers and expats arriving for a first job, transfer, remote-work setup, or family relocation.
  • Short-term newcomers who need a safe first week, even if they will not stay long enough for every resident process.
  • Taiwanese friends, schools, and employers helping a newcomer understand practical arrival steps in plain English.

Make a documents folder before your flight

Do this before you pack. Taiwan offices, banks, schools, carriers, and landlords may ask for different documents, and it is much easier when everything is already in one place.

Document or detail Why it matters Practical note
Passport and visa or entry documents Used for immigration, SIM purchase, school or HR check-in, banking, and many official forms. Keep a printed copy and a phone screenshot. Do not put every copy in checked luggage.
Admission, enrollment, job, or HR documents Helps prove why you are in Taiwan and which office is responsible for you. Students should include admission and enrollment letters. Workers should include offer letter, employment certificate, and HR contact if available.
Chinese address Needed for taxis, delivery, banks, official mail, school records, employer records, and sometimes telecom plans. Ask your dorm, hotel, landlord, school, or employer to send the full Chinese address with room or floor number.
Emergency contacts Useful if your phone is lost, you miss transport, or you need help at a clinic or police station. Save one Taiwan contact if you have one, plus school or HR phone number, housing contact, and family contact.
Money and payment backup Your first month may include deposit, first rent, temporary stay, transport, SIM, bedding, and setup costs. Bring at least one international card plus some cash. Tell your bank you will travel if your card often blocks foreign transactions.
Health and insurance documents Useful before NHI is active or if you need medication, clinic help, or proof of coverage. Bring prescription names, allergy notes, travel insurance, and any school or employer insurance instructions.

Before you arrive checklist

  1. Make one travel document folder. Save your passport, visa or entry permission, admission letter, work contract or offer letter, scholarship letter, housing confirmation, insurance documents, emergency contact, and passport photos. Keep digital copies and printed copies.
  2. Write your first Taiwan address in Chinese. Ask your dorm, hostel, hotel, landlord, school, or employer for the Chinese address. Save it as a screenshot because taxi drivers, delivery staff, banks, and offices may need the Chinese version.
  3. Plan your first airport route. Decide whether you will take Taoyuan Airport MRT, taxi, bus, private pickup, or a school or employer pickup. If you arrive late at night, confirm the last train or prepare taxi money.
  4. Prepare mobile data for landing. Check whether your phone is unlocked and eSIM-ready. If you arrive late or need maps immediately, install a travel eSIM before flying or plan to buy an airport SIM after baggage claim.
  5. Bring more first-month money than a normal monthly budget. Your arrival month may include temporary stay, rent deposit, first rent, bedding, cookware, transport cards, document photos, and emergency cash.
  6. Ask your school or HR for the first office to visit. Students should ask the international office about dorm, ARC, student ID, work permit, NHI, and bank suggestions. Workers should ask HR about payroll bank, employment documents, ARC process, NHI enrollment, and salary records.
  7. Choose temporary housing if you are unsure. A one or two week temporary stay can be safer than signing a long lease before seeing commute time, noise, electricity charges, and whether the address can receive official mail.
  8. Save official sources before you need them. Bookmark the National Immigration Agency, NHIA, Ministry of Labor, Ministry of Education, eTax, Taipei Metro, EasyCard, and your school or employer pages.

First 7 days in Taiwan checklist

  1. Get connected first. Buy or activate a SIM or eSIM, then send your Taiwan number to your school office, employer HR, landlord, and one emergency contact. Ask whether the number can receive SMS for banks and apps.
  2. Buy and top up an EasyCard. You can use it for MRT, many buses, YouBike in many areas after registration, convenience store purchases, and small daily payments. Keep some cash too.
  3. Confirm your housing address and mail plan. Ask for the full Chinese address, room number, building name, landlord or dorm contact, and how official mail is received. Take photos of the room condition before moving in.
  4. Visit the office that owns your case. Students should visit the international office or department office. Workers should visit HR. Bring your passport, entry documents, address, phone number, and questions about ARC, NHI, banking, and deadlines.
  5. Start the bank account route. Do not walk into a random small branch first. Try your employer payroll bank, your school's recommended branch, or a larger branch near a university, business district, or workplace.
  6. Learn your convenience store basics. 7-Eleven, FamilyMart, Hi-Life, and OK Mart can help with EasyCard top-up, bill payment, printing, parcel pickup, ATM access, and simple food when you are not settled yet.
  7. Create a small emergency card. Save your Chinese address, school or HR phone number, landlord or dorm contact, embassy or representative office, health insurance status, allergies, and emergency contacts in your phone and wallet.

What to ask school or employer in your first meeting

Bring your passport, Taiwan phone number, Chinese address, and document folder. Ask direct questions so you do not miss deadlines.

  • Students: ask which office handles ARC, student ID, course registration, dorm, NHI, work permit, scholarship payment, and school-recommended bank branches.
  • Workers: ask HR who handles ARC, work permit if relevant, NHI, payroll bank, salary date, payslips, tax withholding, address records, and emergency contact forms.
  • Everyone: ask which email address or online system you must check for official notices. Missing school, HR, immigration, or tax mail can create problems later.
  • If you do not understand a document: ask for the deadline, the office responsible, and whether there is an English version or someone who can explain the Chinese text.

First 30 days in Taiwan checklist

  1. Confirm your ARC or resident certificate route. Your route and timing depend on your visa, student status, work status, and other factors. Ask your school or employer which documents you need and verify the official NIA instructions.
  2. Ask when NHI applies to you. National Health Insurance timing may depend on your residence status and category. Do not assume you are covered until your school, employer, or NHIA confirms enrollment.
  3. Finish or schedule your bank account. Bring passport, ARC or resident certificate if available, Taiwan phone number, Chinese address, proof of enrollment or employment, and extra ID if you have it. If one branch refuses, ask what document is missing and try the recommended branch.
  4. Review your housing cost honestly. Confirm deposit, first rent, electricity rate, water, internet, management fee, trash rules, cooking rules, guest rules, lease end date, and whether official mail can arrive there.
  5. Build a realistic monthly budget. Use the cost calculator after you know your housing, transport, phone, food, and setup costs. Taipei and New Taipei can feel very different once commute time is included.
  6. Set up your daily transport routine. Test MRT, bus, Taiwan Railway, YouBike, and walking time during the real hours you will commute. A cheap room can become expensive if it costs time and taxis.
  7. Create a tax and document folder. Save salary records, payslips, withholding statements, rent receipts, school payment records, and entry or exit dates. This helps later even if you are not filing immediately.
  8. Report unclear or outdated information. If a rule, link, or office step on this site looks wrong, send feedback so the guide can be corrected before other newcomers rely on it.

Realistic examples

International student arriving for university: Maya lands at Taoyuan Airport on a Sunday evening. Before flying, she saved her dorm address in Chinese, installed a travel eSIM, and packed her admission letter, passport, visa page, and printed dorm confirmation. On Monday, she visits the international office, asks about ARC and NHI timing, buys an EasyCard, and uses the school housing board before looking at 591 listings.
Foreign worker arriving for a first job: Daniel starts work in Taipei next week. HR tells him the payroll bank and gives him an employment letter, office address in Chinese, and contact person. He uses a short prepaid SIM first, stays in temporary housing for two weeks, checks commute time during rush hour, then opens a bank account at the payroll branch instead of trying a random branch near his hotel.

Common mistakes

  • Arriving with no local data plan. Airport Wi-Fi helps, but you still need maps, messaging, translation, and emergency contact after you leave the terminal.
  • Only saving the English address. A Chinese address is often easier for taxis, delivery, mail, banks, and office forms.
  • Signing a lease before checking commute and fees. Visit in person when possible, check electricity rate, deposit, mail access, noise, cooking rules, and commute at real hours.
  • Assuming one friend's rule applies to you. ARC, NHI, work permit, tax, and bank requirements can change by status, school, employer, branch, and date.
  • Trying the wrong bank branch first. Start with the school-recommended branch or employer payroll bank because staff are more likely to know the document path.
  • Ignoring official mail. Ask where ARC notices, bank letters, NHI mail, school mail, employer mail, and tax notices will be sent.
  • Not keeping records. Save receipts, rent payments, deposit records, salary records, HR emails, school emails, and official application screenshots.

What to do next

  1. Choose your first SIM or eSIM plan before you worry about long-term contracts.
  2. Estimate your first-month and monthly budget with a deposit and setup buffer.
  3. Learn EasyCard, MRT, bus, and YouBike basics before choosing housing far from school or work.
  4. Read the renting guide before paying a deposit or signing a lease.
  5. Use the banking guide to prepare documents and choose a branch strategy.
  6. Check ARC basics if you will stay long term or have resident status.
  7. Check NHI basics before assuming you are covered.
  8. Read the tax guide and keep salary or income records early.
