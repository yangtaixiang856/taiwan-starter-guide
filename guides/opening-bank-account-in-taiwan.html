Banking guide

How do foreigners open a bank account in Taiwan?

The hardest part is rarely the form. It is choosing the right branch and bringing enough proof the first time.

Last updated 2026-05-27 Official and bank sources checked 2026-05-21 Students, expats, workers Practical checklist

Short answer

Start with your employer payroll bank or school-recommended branch. Bring your passport, ARC or resident certificate if you have one, Taiwan phone number, Chinese address, student or employment proof, and extra ID if available. If one branch refuses, try a bigger branch near a university, business district, or your workplace.

Who this guide is for

  • International students who need a Taiwan account for scholarship, tuition refunds, rent, daily expenses, or part-time work income.
  • Expats and white-collar workers who need payroll, rent transfers, debit card payments, online banking, or ATM access.
  • Newcomers who were rejected by one branch and need a better strategy before trying again.
  • Taiwanese friends, school offices, or HR teams helping a foreigner prepare documents.

Step-by-step checklist

  1. Get a Taiwan phone number first. Many banks need SMS verification for online banking, app setup, fraud checks, and customer contact.
  2. Prepare your Chinese address. Bring a dorm certificate, rental contract, employer housing document, school letter, or a clear written address in Chinese.
  3. Choose the right first branch. Try your employer payroll bank, your school-recommended branch, campus bank, or a larger branch near a university, office district, science park, or your workplace.
  4. Bring more documents than you think you need. Passport, ARC or resident certificate if available, student or employment proof, Taiwan phone number, Chinese address, and extra ID can reduce back-and-forth.
  5. Explain the account purpose clearly. Say whether it is for salary, scholarship, rent, tuition refunds, daily expenses, or long-term living in Taiwan.
  6. Ask about online banking and debit card setup. Confirm whether you can receive SMS, use the app in English, transfer money, withdraw at ATMs, and set transaction limits.
  7. If rejected, ask what is missing. Write down the missing document or reason, then try the school or payroll branch instead of arguing at the counter.
  8. Keep your account safe. Never lend your passbook, debit card, online banking login, or SIM card to another person.

Which bank should you try first?

There is no bank that is always easiest for every foreigner. The branch matters as much as the bank name. Use this order:

  • If you have a job: ask HR which bank handles payroll. A payroll account is usually easier because the employer can provide a contact person and employment proof.
  • If you are a student: ask the international office or department office for the exact bank branch foreign students usually use. Some schools have a campus bank, a nearby partner branch, or a post office route.
  • If you want English-facing information: check Cathay United Bank's foreign customer page and E.SUN Bank's appointment page before visiting.
  • If you are near a university or science park: try larger branches that regularly handle foreign students, researchers, and international employees.
  • If a random branch says no: ask politely what document is missing, then try another branch with better foreign-customer experience.

Example: An engineer in Hsinchu first walks into a small branch near his apartment and is told to bring more documents. The next day, HR sends him to the company's payroll branch with an employment certificate and office phone number. The second visit is much smoother.

Documents to bring

Bring originals and a few paper copies if you can. Branches may ask different follow-up questions, so over-preparing saves time.

  • Passport: original passport, ideally with a copy of the photo page.
  • ARC or resident certificate: if you have it. If you do not yet have it, ask whether the branch accepts another official ID number document or whether you should wait.
  • Taiwan phone number: many banks need it for SMS verification and online banking setup.
  • Chinese address: rental contract, dorm certificate, employer dorm address, or a school/employer letter can help.
  • Proof of purpose: admission letter, enrollment certificate, student ID, employment certificate, work permit, salary letter, scholarship letter, or payroll request.
  • Second ID if available: student ID, employee ID, NHI card, foreign driver's license, or another official photo ID. The branch decides what it can accept.
  • Tax and home-country details: some banks ask for tax residency, home address, occupation, and source of funds because of anti-money-laundering rules.
  • Small opening deposit: ask the branch how much cash is needed.
  • Name seal or signature: many foreigners use signatures, but some branches may still ask about a seal. Ask before going if you are unsure.

What to say at the branch

Be specific. A vague "I want an account" can trigger more questions. Explain your practical reason:

  • "I need a salary account for this employer."
  • "I am an international student and need an account for scholarship, rent, and daily expenses."
  • "I live in Taiwan long term and need an account for rent, utilities, and card payments."

Example: A student brings passport, ARC, student certificate, dorm address in Chinese, Taiwan phone number, and a note from the international office. The banker still asks why she needs the account, so she says it is for tuition refunds, scholarship payments, rent, and daily expenses.

Common mistakes and problems

  • No ARC yet: many branches prefer an ARC or resident certificate. Ask your school or employer whether there is a branch that can help before the ARC is issued.
  • No Taiwan phone number: get at least a prepaid SIM first, because SMS verification is common.
  • Address not clear: bring the address in Chinese and proof that you live there, such as dorm confirmation or rental contract.
  • Online account opening not available: foreigners often need an in-person branch visit, even if the bank advertises online services.
  • Branch refuses without clear reason: ask what document is missing and try another branch. Do not argue at the counter; branch risk checks are strict.
  • Using only an English address: banks often need the Chinese address exactly as it appears in local documents or mail.
  • Letting someone else control the account: never let a broker, employer, landlord, or friend keep your bank card, passbook, app login, or password.

Safety reminders

Do not lend or sell your bank account, debit card, online banking login, or SIM card. If someone says they need your account to receive money, move money, or help with a job, treat it as a serious risk.

Also avoid letting an employer, broker, landlord, or friend keep your bank card or online banking password. Your salary and identity documents should stay under your control.

Bank-specific notes

These are practical starting points, not guarantees. Requirements can change and branch staff may ask for more documents.

  • Cathay United Bank: has an English foreign customer page and branch information. It is worth checking before visiting.
  • E.SUN Bank: has an English account-opening appointment page that mentions non-ROC natural persons. Appointment pages can help you prepare documents before showing up.
  • Employer payroll banks: often work best for employees because HR already has a process.
  • School-recommended banks or post offices: often work best for students because the school can tell you the exact branch and documents.

What to do next

  1. If you do not have a Taiwan number yet, read the SIM and eSIM guide first.
  2. Ask your school international office or employer HR which branch foreigners usually use.
  3. Prepare your document folder: passport, ARC or resident certificate if available, Chinese address proof, Taiwan phone number, student or employment proof, and extra ID.
  4. Before visiting, call or message the branch if possible and ask what documents a non-ROC national should bring.
  5. After opening the account, save your bank name, branch, account number, online banking login method, card limits, and customer service number in a secure place.
