What is the 183-day tax rule in Taiwan?

The 183-day threshold is one of the first concepts foreigners hear about, but it is not the only tax question.

Updated 2026-05-20 Official sources checked 2026-05-20 Tax residency basics Check eTax before filing

Short answer

Staying in Taiwan for 183 days or more in a taxable year is a major threshold for tax residency. Your income type, withholding, deductions, and documents still matter.

How to think about it

Count days carefully, then ask a second question: what income are you reporting? A student with part-time wages, a local employee, and a short-term contractor can have different filing questions.

What the official eTax guidance points to

eTax explains alien individual income tax using the number of days stayed in Taiwan during the taxable year. In plain English, this means you should first count your Taiwan days for January 1 to December 31, then check how your income was paid and withheld.

  • Not more than 90 days, over 90 but less than 183 days, and 183 days or longer are treated as separate situations.
  • People who stay 183 days or longer are generally discussed under resident filing rules.
  • People under 183 days may still have Taiwan-source income questions, so do not stop at the day count.
  • Use your passport stamps, travel records, school or work dates, and income records when asking the tax office.

Realistic example

Someone stays in Taiwan for 120 days on a short assignment and receives Taiwan-source salary. Another person stays 210 days as a local employee. Their tax residency and withholding questions may be very different, even if both are foreign nationals.

How to avoid a bad estimate

Do not count by memory. Make a simple spreadsheet with arrival dates, departure dates, and nights outside Taiwan. If your travel pattern is messy, bring that list to the tax office and ask them to verify the count before you rely on it.

Example: A remote worker thinks he stayed "about six months," but two short trips outside Taiwan put him below 183 days. His filing route may be different from a coworker who stayed continuously from February to December.

What not to assume

  • Do not assume less than 183 days means no filing questions.
  • Do not assume more than 183 days means your employer handled everything.
  • Do not rely on a friend's refund experience without checking your own facts.

Reminder

Rules may change. This guide is educational, not tax advice. Check Ministry of Finance/eTax guidance or ask a local tax office before making decisions.
