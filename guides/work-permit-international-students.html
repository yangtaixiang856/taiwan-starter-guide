Do international students need a work permit in Taiwan?

If you plan to do paid part-time work as a student, slow down and verify the rules before your first shift.

Updated 2026-05-22 Official sources checked 2026-05-22 Labor-sensitive topic Educational only

Short answer

Many international students need a valid work permit before doing paid work in Taiwan. The school usually helps confirm documents and student status, but the official labor authority rules decide. Work-hour limits, eligibility, documents, and application steps may change, so check Workforce Development Agency and school guidance before accepting a job.

Important disclaimer

This is educational information only. It is not legal, immigration, labor, employment, or tax advice. Work permit rules may change. Your exact eligibility depends on your student status, program, documents, job type, and official labor rules. Check official sources and your school before working.

Who this guide is for

  • International degree students considering part-time work.
  • Exchange or language students asking whether they can work.
  • Students offered cafe, tutoring, research assistant, internship, or campus jobs.
  • Students who want to ask the school the right questions before applying.

Step-by-step checklist

  1. Ask your school first. Start with the international office, student affairs office, or department office. Ask whether your student category can apply.
  2. Confirm the official system. Many applications use the Workforce Development Agency online work permit system, but your school may have an internal review step first.
  3. Prepare student-status documents. Ask whether you need enrollment proof, student ID, passport, ARC, transcript, school approval, or other documents.
  4. Check work-hour limits. Official guidance has described limits during semesters and exceptions for winter and summer vacation, but rules can change. Verify the current rule before scheduling shifts.
  5. Wait for approval before starting paid work. A verbal promise from an employer is not enough.
  6. Keep records. Save permit approval, job offer, work schedule, payslips, bank deposits, and messages about shifts.
  7. Renew or stop before expiry. Put the permit expiry date in your calendar and ask the school what to do before it expires.

Realistic example

Example: A student gets a weekend cafe job offer near campus. The manager says she can start this Saturday, but she has not received a work permit yet. She asks the international office which documents to upload, waits for official approval, then gives the employer the permit information before her first paid shift.

Common mistakes

  • Starting work before approval. Do not rely on "everyone does it" advice.
  • Assuming all student categories are the same. Degree, exchange, language, research, and internship situations may differ.
  • Ignoring work-hour limits. Keep a weekly schedule record and confirm whether vacation rules apply to your case.
  • Forgetting the expiry date. A valid permit today may not be valid next semester.
  • Not saving proof of salary and hours. You may need records for tax, disputes, renewals, or school questions.
  • Mixing work permit and tax questions. Permission to work and tax filing are connected in real life, but they are not the same process.

What to ask your school or employer

  • Am I eligible to apply for a student work permit under my current program?
  • Which office reviews my application before it goes to the official system?
  • What documents should I upload?
  • What is the current work-hour limit during the semester and during vacation?
  • Can this job type or internship be covered by my permit?
  • What should I do before the permit expires?
  • Will the employer provide payslips and salary records?

What to do next

  1. Send your school a clear email with your student ID, program, job type, and expected start date.
  2. Ask for the official link and the document checklist for your student category.
  3. Do not start paid work until approval is clear.
  4. Save your work permit approval and expiry date.
  5. Track hours, salary, deductions, and payslips every month.
