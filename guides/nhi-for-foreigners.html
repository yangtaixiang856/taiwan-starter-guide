How does National Health Insurance work for foreigners?

Taiwan NHI can be very helpful, but your enrollment route depends on your residence status, work or school situation, and timing.

Updated 2026-05-22 Official sources checked 2026-05-22 Health-insurance-sensitive topic Educational only

Short answer

Foreigners may be eligible for Taiwan National Health Insurance depending on residence status, employment, student category, dependent status, and timing. Some people enroll through an employer, some through a school, some through a local office or other insured unit. Rules may change, so check official NHIA sources before assuming you are covered.

Important disclaimer

This is educational information only. It is not medical, legal, employment, or insurance advice. NHI eligibility, enrollment start dates, premium rules, card procedures, and clinic handling may change. Official NHIA information and your actual insured status prevail.

Who this guide is for

  • International students asking whether the school handles NHI enrollment.
  • Foreign employees checking whether HR has enrolled them correctly.
  • Dependents or family members who need to ask which insured unit applies.
  • Newcomers who need to visit a clinic before their physical NHI card arrives.

Step-by-step checklist

  1. Identify your status. Student, employee, dependent, person without a fixed employer, and other categories may use different enrollment routes.
  2. Ask who is responsible for enrollment. Employees usually start with HR. Students start with the international office or school insurance office. Dependents should ask the family member's insured unit or NHIA.
  3. Ask for the expected coverage start date. Do not assume coverage is active just because paperwork was submitted.
  4. Ask what documents you can use before the card arrives. If the physical card is still being processed, NHIA has guidance about temporary medical care documents.
  5. Keep premium and enrollment records. Save school emails, HR emails, application proof, payment records, and card pickup notices.
  6. Update the right office when your situation changes. Job changes, school changes, graduation, address changes, or dependent changes may affect your route.

Realistic example

Example: A foreign employee gets sick during the first week of work. HR says the NHI enrollment has been submitted, but the clinic cannot see the active card yet. The employee asks HR for the enrollment date and application proof, then checks NHIA guidance on what can be shown before the physical card arrives.

Common mistakes

  • Thinking "ARC means NHI is automatic." Residence documents and insurance enrollment are related, but not the same thing.
  • Not asking the start date. A gap between arrival, employment, enrollment, and card arrival can create confusion at clinics.
  • Losing the application proof. Keep screenshots and emails until the card works.
  • Ignoring job or school changes. Your insured unit may change when you change jobs, graduate, transfer schools, or become a dependent.
  • Relying on an old forum answer. NHI rules and pages may change. Check official NHIA information.

What to ask your school or employer

  • Am I already enrolled in NHI? If not, when will enrollment start?
  • Which office or insured unit is responsible for me?
  • What documents should I bring to a clinic before my NHI card arrives?
  • How are premiums paid or deducted?
  • What should I do if I change address, job, school, or dependent status?
  • Which official NHIA page should I read for my case?

What to do next

  1. Save your enrollment confirmation, card application proof, and premium records.
  2. Ask your school or HR to confirm the active date in writing.
  3. Before your first clinic visit, prepare passport, ARC, NHI card if available, and any temporary proof your school or employer gives you.
  4. Save the nearest clinic, hospital, and emergency contact in your phone.
  5. Check NHIA official pages when you change job, school, or residence status.

Official sources to verify

Rules may change. Check official sources. Needs manual verification before publishing major updates: eligibility category, six-month residence logic, employer route, student route, premium handling, and clinic document rules.
