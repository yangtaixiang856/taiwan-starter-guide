What should I check before renting a room in Taiwan?

Renting is often the biggest cost and the easiest place for newcomers to make expensive mistakes, especially when the listing is in Chinese and the real cost is hidden in fees.

Last updated 2026-05-27 Official sources checked 2026-05-20 Shared rooms and apartments Practical checklist

Short answer

Use school housing boards, 591, Rakuya, Tealit, Facebook groups, and local agents to compare options. Before paying anything, check deposit, electricity rate, management fee, internet, trash rules, visitor rules, early termination, and whether the address can receive official mail.

Who this guide is for

  • International students looking for a dorm alternative, shared room, studio, or short first-month stay.
  • Expats and white-collar workers comparing Taipei, New Taipei, Taoyuan, Hsinchu, Taichung, Tainan, or Kaohsiung housing.
  • Newcomers who need a Taiwan address for bank visits, delivery apps, school records, employer HR, ARC notices, or official mail.
  • Taiwanese friends helping a foreigner check a Chinese lease, rental platform, electricity fee, or suspicious deposit request.

Where foreigners usually search for rentals

No single platform is best for everyone. Use at least two sources so you can compare price, location, photos, and landlord behavior.

  • School housing office or off-campus housing board: best first stop for students. Listings may be closer to campus and the office may know which landlords have rented to foreign students before.
  • 591 Rent: the largest local rental platform. It has many listings, but most are in Chinese and some landlords may not reply in English.
  • Rakuya: another large Taiwan rental platform. Useful for cross-checking prices and seeing whether the same room appears elsewhere.
  • Facebook groups: useful for shared rooms, English posts, and short-term housing. Be careful with fake listings, copied photos, and pressure to transfer deposits quickly.
  • Tealit and expat listing boards: smaller inventory, but sometimes easier for English-speaking roommates or Taipei shared apartments.
  • Local real-estate agents: useful if you need help in Chinese or want a full apartment, but ask clearly about agent fees before viewing.

Example: A new student checks her university housing board first, then uses 591 to understand normal prices near the MRT, and finally posts in a Facebook group asking for roommates near campus. She compares all three instead of trusting the first nice photo.

Chinese rental words worth knowing

  • 租金: monthly rent.
  • 押金: deposit, often one or two months, but confirm the exact return conditions.
  • 管理費: building management fee.
  • 電費 / 水費: electricity and water fees.
  • 獨立套房: private studio with private bathroom.
  • 雅房: private room with shared bathroom.
  • 分租套房: subdivided rental unit; check soundproofing, ventilation, and safety carefully.
  • 可開伙: cooking allowed.
  • 可養寵物: pets allowed.
  • 可入戶籍 / 可收信: address registration or receiving mail may be allowed, but ask what that means for your exact situation.

Step-by-step checklist before paying a deposit

  1. Choose your real commute zone. Check the route to school or work during the hours you will actually travel. A cheap room can become expensive if you need taxis or a long bus transfer every day.
  2. Search at least two places. Compare school housing boards, 591, Rakuya, Tealit, Facebook groups, and agents before deciding what a normal price looks like.
  3. Calculate the real monthly cost. Add rent, management fee, electricity, water, internet, trash bags or trash service, laundry, commute, and any agent fee.
  4. Confirm who is taking your money. Ask whether the person is the landlord, agent, current tenant, or roommate. If it is an agent, ask the agent fee in writing before viewing.
  5. View the room or ask for a live video. Do not rely only on beautiful photos. Ask the person to show the window, bathroom, air conditioner, washing machine, entrance, and building hallway.
  6. Ask about the address. Confirm the Chinese address, room number, whether official mail can arrive there, and whether you can use the address for school, bank, employer, or ARC-related mail if needed.
  7. Check electricity carefully. Ask whether electricity is billed by Taipower bill, private meter, fixed rate per kWh, or included in rent. Ask to see how it is calculated.
  8. Read the lease before paying. Check deposit return, early termination, repair responsibility, subletting, visitors, cooking, pets, smoking, and cleaning rules.
  9. Pay in a traceable way. Keep bank transfer records, receipts, LINE messages, screenshots, and the landlord or agent's name.
  10. Take move-in photos. Photograph walls, floor, furniture, appliances, keys, meter readings, bathroom, and existing damage before unpacking.

Official source check

This page was checked against Ministry of the Interior rental electricity guidance and government sample residential lease resources on 2026-05-20. The most important newcomer lesson is simple: do not treat a chat message as your whole rental agreement.

  • Ask for a written lease and compare it with a government sample residential lease agreement.
  • If electricity is billed by usage, ask how the per-kWh price is calculated and whether the landlord can show the bill basis.
  • For new residential lease contracts under the updated MOI electricity billing rules, landlords should not charge more than the current average electricity price shown on the electricity bill when usage-based billing applies.
  • If there is a dispute about hidden electricity charges, rent increases, or lease termination, ask the local government or Legal Aid Foundation where to get help.

Realistic example

Someone renting a shared room in New Taipei sees a cheap NT$8,000 listing, but electricity is charged separately, the room has no window, the washing machine is shared by eight people, and the nearest MRT is a 20-minute bus ride away. The real monthly cost and daily stress are not as low as the listing suggests.

Common mistake

Do not compare rent only by the headline price. A cheaper room with high electricity fees, no kitchen, no window, weak phone signal, and a long commute can cost more in real life.

Another common mistake is paying a deposit before seeing the room, confirming the landlord or agent identity, and receiving a written receipt. If someone pressures you to transfer money immediately because "many people are waiting," slow down.

Questions to ask the landlord

  • Can I use this address for ARC, school, bank, or official mail if needed?
  • How much is the deposit, and when can it be deducted or returned?
  • What exact electricity rate will I pay, and can I see the bill or meter method?
  • Is the listed rent the final monthly cost, or are there management, cleaning, trash, internet, or common-area fees?
  • Who pays for repairs if the air conditioner, water heater, or washing machine breaks?
  • What happens if I need to move out early because my school, job, or visa situation changes?
  • Can I take photos or a video during move-in to record the room condition?

Example: A student signs quickly because the room is near campus, then learns the landlord does not allow official mail at the address. Asking this before paying the deposit would have saved a second move.

What to do next

  1. Pick 2 or 3 districts or MRT stations that fit your school or work commute.
  2. Search one official or semi-official source first, such as your school housing board, then compare 591, Rakuya, Tealit, and Facebook groups.
  3. Make a short message in English and Chinese asking rent, deposit, electricity, management fee, lease length, and official mail.
  4. Before paying a deposit, ask a Taiwanese friend, school office, HR contact, or trusted local person to review the Chinese lease if you do not understand it.
  5. Use the cost of living calculator after you know the rent, deposit, and commute cost.

Simple message to send before viewing

You can copy this and adjust it:

Hi, I am interested in this room. Is it still available? I am a foreign student/worker. Could you confirm the monthly rent, deposit, electricity rate, management fee, lease length, nearest MRT or bus stop, and whether official mail can be received at this address?

If you write in Chinese, include the key words: 租金, 押金, 電費, 管理費, 租期, 可收信.

Reminder

Rules and local practice may change. This page is practical housing information, not legal advice. For serious disputes, contact the local land administration office, consumer protection channel, or Legal Aid Foundation.
