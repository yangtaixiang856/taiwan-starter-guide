What is an ARC or resident certificate in Taiwan?

The ARC is one of the documents that makes everyday life easier for many long-term foreigners in Taiwan, but the route depends on your exact status.

Updated 2026-05-22 Official sources checked 2026-05-22 Immigration-sensitive topic Educational only

Short answer

An Alien Resident Certificate, often called an ARC, is a residence document many foreign students, workers, dependents, and long-term residents use in Taiwan. It can help with banking, phone plans, NHI enrollment, work or school administration, and official paperwork. Rules may change, so check National Immigration Agency information and your school or employer before applying or renewing.

Important disclaimer

This is educational information only. It is not legal, immigration, or professional advice. ARC rules, documents, fees, processing times, address-change rules, and renewal rules may change. Official National Immigration Agency instructions and your actual case details prevail.

Who this guide is for

  • International students preparing for a student ARC or residence extension.
  • Foreign employees and expats trying to understand why employers or banks ask for an ARC.
  • Dependents or family members who need to ask the right office about their residence route.
  • Newcomers who have heard "get your ARC first" but are not sure what that means.

Step-by-step checklist

  1. Identify your category. Student, employee, dependent, entrepreneur, researcher, and other categories can have different forms and documents.
  2. Ask which official route applies. Students should ask the international office. Employees should ask HR. Do not assume your friend's route applies to you.
  3. Prepare identity and status documents. This may include passport, visa or entry record, admission or enrollment proof, employment documents, address information, photos, and other category-specific items.
  4. Use the official online system or service center route for your case. Some student processes use the NIA online application system. Other categories may use different service channels.
  5. Keep receipts and screenshots. Save application numbers, payment proof, email notices, and upload confirmations.
  6. Check the card details when issued. Name, birth date, ARC number, address, and validity dates should be checked quickly.
  7. Track renewals and changes. Put your expiration date in your calendar, and ask NIA or your school/employer what to do if you move, change school, change job, or leave Taiwan.

Realistic example

Example: A master's student needs to open a bank account, but the branch asks for passport, ARC, Taiwan phone number, Chinese address, and proof of enrollment. She has not received the physical card yet, so she brings the NIA application receipt, school enrollment certificate, dorm address in Chinese, and asks her international office which branch usually accepts students in that situation.

Common mistakes

  • Waiting until the last week. Applications, payments, document fixes, and card pickup can take time.
  • Using the wrong category. A student, employee, and dependent may need different documents.
  • Forgetting address changes. Address rules can be sensitive. Ask NIA how and when to update your address after moving.
  • Ignoring name format. Your passport name, school record, employer record, bank record, and ARC record should be consistent as much as possible.
  • Relying only on old blog posts or social media. Use them for context, then verify with official sources.

What to ask your school or employer

  • Which ARC category should I use for my status?
  • Which documents should I prepare, and do they need Chinese versions?
  • Is there a school or company contact person if NIA asks questions?
  • What should I do if my address changes?
  • When should I start renewal before the expiry date?
  • Can you confirm the official NIA link I should use?

What to do next

  1. Save the NIA official page and your application portal link.
  2. Create a folder for passport scans, admission or employment documents, address proof, application receipts, and payment records.
  3. Set calendar reminders for application, pickup, address update, and renewal dates.
  4. If a bank, phone store, landlord, or clinic asks for an ARC, ask exactly which document or number they need.
  5. Before taking action, check the current NIA instructions or ask your school/employer office.
