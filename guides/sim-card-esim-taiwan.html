How do I get a SIM card or eSIM in Taiwan?

Your first Taiwan phone plan should solve the first-week problem: maps, messaging, transport, housing calls, and bank SMS.

Last updated 2026-05-27 Carrier sources checked 2026-05-22 Students, expats, workers Airport arrival friendly

Short answer

If you need internet immediately, buy a tourist SIM or eSIM at the airport, or install a travel eSIM before landing. Use that first plan for 7 to 30 days. After you have a stable address, ARC or school/employer documents, and know your signal quality, compare long-term prepaid or monthly plans.

Who this guide is for

  • International students arriving at Taoyuan Airport, Songshan Airport, or Kaohsiung Airport.
  • Expats and white-collar workers who need a Taiwan number for housing, banking, food delivery, and work setup.
  • Foreign workers and newcomers who want a simple first plan before choosing a longer mobile contract.
  • People deciding between physical SIM, local eSIM, and travel eSIM options.

Step-by-step: get connected without overcommitting

  1. Before your flight, check if your phone is unlocked. If your phone is locked to your home-country carrier, a Taiwan SIM may not work. Also check whether your phone supports eSIM.
  2. Decide if you need a Taiwan phone number. Some travel eSIMs are data-only. They are fine for maps and messaging apps, but a Taiwan number can help with landlords, banks, delivery, YouBike, and verification SMS.
  3. At the airport, choose a short first plan. Airport counters usually sell tourist prepaid SIM or eSIM packages. Bring your passport and confirm plan length, data amount, local calls, SMS, hotspot use, and pickup or activation steps.
  4. Keep your first plan simple. For the first week, your goal is not the cheapest possible plan. Your goal is to safely reach housing, contact people, use maps, and solve arrival paperwork.
  5. After you settle, compare longer options. Visit carrier stores near your school, office, or apartment. Ask about prepaid top-up, monthly plans, contract length, deposit, cancellation fees, and whether an ARC is required.
  6. Test signal where you actually live. A plan that works well at Taipei Main Station may be weak in your basement room, mountain town, factory dorm, or rural work site.
  7. Do not change numbers too often. Your Taiwan phone number may become connected to banks, LINE, delivery apps, school systems, work accounts, and two-factor login.

Airport arrival route

For many newcomers, the easiest first move is this: land, pass immigration, pick up baggage, find a telecom counter in the arrivals area, and buy a short prepaid tourist SIM or eSIM with your passport.

Ask these exact questions at the counter:

  • "Does this plan include a Taiwan phone number?"
  • "Can I use hotspot sharing?"
  • "How many days is it valid?"
  • "Can I top it up later?"
  • "Can I switch to a monthly plan after I get my ARC?"
  • "Will this work in my city or near my school or workplace?"

Realistic example: An exchange student lands at Taoyuan Airport at 7 p.m. She buys a 7-day tourist SIM with a Taiwan number, uses Google Maps to reach her hostel, messages the dorm office on LINE, and calls a landlord the next day. After she gets her dorm address and student documents, she compares longer prepaid plans near campus.

Starter recommendations without affiliate links

These are practical routes, not paid recommendations. Prices and terms change, so confirm the latest details with the carrier before buying.

  • If you arrive late or alone: install a travel eSIM before flying, then decide later whether you need a Taiwan number. This keeps maps and messaging alive even if counters are closed.
  • If you stay less than two weeks: an airport tourist SIM or eSIM may be enough. Confirm data amount, local calls, SMS, hotspot, and expiry date.
  • If you are a student for one semester or more: use an airport plan for the first 7 to 15 days, then compare prepaid or monthly plans near campus after you have your school documents and address.
  • If you are starting a job: ask HR whether your Taiwan number will be used for payroll, banking, NHI, or internal systems before changing numbers.
  • If you need banking soon: choose a plan that can receive Taiwan SMS, because many banks use SMS verification.

SIM, eSIM, prepaid, or monthly plan?

  • Physical SIM: simple and widely available. Good if your phone has a SIM slot and you want staff to help install it.
  • Local eSIM: convenient if your phone supports eSIM. Ask the carrier whether the plan includes voice calls and SMS, not just data.
  • Travel eSIM: good before landing if you arrive late or want backup data immediately. Many are data-only, so they may not solve Taiwan phone-number needs.
  • Prepaid plan: flexible for the first month or first semester. You top up or renew when needed.
  • Monthly contract: may be cheaper or stronger long term, but check contract length, deposit, cancellation fee, and required documents before signing.

What to compare before paying

  1. Local phone number: ask whether the plan includes a Taiwan number or only data.
  2. SMS: ask whether you can receive local SMS for banking, delivery, school, and app verification.
  3. Hotspot: ask whether laptop tethering is allowed if your room does not have internet yet.
  4. Plan length: write down the expiry date so service does not stop during housing, banking, or school setup.
  5. Top-up method: ask whether you can renew at convenience stores, telecom stores, carrier apps, or online.
  6. Documents: tourist SIMs may use passport-based purchase, while long-term plans may ask for ARC, address, deposit, or other checks.
  7. Cancellation cost: do not sign a long monthly plan until you understand contract length, early termination, and deposit.
  8. Signal where you live: test inside your apartment, dorm, basement room, office, classroom, or factory dorm before committing long term.

Common mistakes

  • Buying a long contract before seeing your apartment. Test signal at home, school, and work first.
  • Assuming every eSIM has a phone number. Some eSIMs are data-only and cannot receive local SMS.
  • Forgetting bank SMS. If you open a bank account, ask whether your phone number must stay active for online banking verification.
  • Not checking hotspot rules. If you need laptop internet before home Wi-Fi is installed, ask whether hotspot sharing is allowed.
  • Changing numbers after giving them to everyone. Update school, HR, landlord, bank, hospital, and delivery apps if your number changes.

What to do next

  1. Save your Taiwan number in your phone and notes app.
  2. Send the number to your school office, employer, landlord, and emergency contact if needed.
  3. Write down the plan end date so you do not lose service unexpectedly.
  4. Once you have ARC or school/employer documents, compare prepaid top-up and monthly options.
  5. Before opening a bank account, make sure your number can receive SMS in Taiwan.
