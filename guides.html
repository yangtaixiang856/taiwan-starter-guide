Before arrival

What should I prepare before moving to Taiwan?

Short answer: Prepare documents, first-month money, a Chinese address, temporary mobile data, housing backup, airport transport, and a clear plan for school or HR, banking, ARC, NHI, and official mail.

Most first-week stress comes from simple gaps: no Taiwan number, no cash, no Chinese address, no EasyCard, or missing school and work documents. Keep digital copies and printed copies because some offices still prefer paper.

Realistic example: A student lands in Taoyuan on Sunday night. Her dorm opens Monday, but she already has an eSIM, NT$8,000 cash, her admission letter, and the dorm address in Chinese, so she can take a taxi and message the housing office.

Arrival

How do I get a SIM or eSIM in Taiwan?

Short answer: Use an airport SIM, tourist SIM, or travel eSIM for the first week. Compare longer prepaid or monthly plans after you know your address, documents, and signal quality.

Your first plan should solve maps, messaging, local calls, housing contact, and bank SMS. Ask whether the plan includes a Taiwan number, SMS, hotspot, top-up, and whether a long-term plan requires an ARC or deposit.

Realistic example: A new exchange student buys a 7-day airport SIM, uses it to find housing, then switches to a monthly plan near campus after getting a local student address.

Immigration

What is an ARC or resident certificate?

Short answer: An Alien Resident Certificate is a key residence document many long-term foreigners need for banking, phones, work, insurance, and everyday administration.

Your visa type affects the route. Students, employees, dependents, and other residents may have different forms, deadlines, and required documents. Keep your address and contact details current.

Realistic example: A master's student needs a bank account, but the branch asks for a passport, ARC, Taiwan phone number, and proof of enrollment. Having the ARC receipt and school certificate helps the school office explain the situation.

Reminder: Immigration rules may change. This is educational information only. Check the National Immigration Agency or your school/employer office before making visa or residence decisions.

Health

How does National Health Insurance work for foreigners?

Short answer: Eligibility depends on your residence status and category. Students, employees, dependents, and workers may enroll through different routes.

Do not rely on secondhand rules from old forum posts. Ask your school, employer, or local NHI office which route applies to your status, then keep proof of enrollment and premium payments.

Realistic example: A foreign employee assumes HR enrolled him immediately, but the clinic says his card is not active. He asks HR for the enrollment date and keeps the email for the next visit.

Reminder: NHI rules may change and eligibility can depend on your exact status. This is educational information only. Check official NHIA sources before assuming you are covered.

Housing

What should I check before renting a room in Taiwan?

Short answer: Start with school housing boards, 591, Rakuya, Tealit, Facebook rental groups, and local agents, then check deposit, electricity fee, management fee, commute, Chinese address, and whether official mail can arrive there.

Housing is where newcomers lose the most money. 591 has the most local listings but is mostly Chinese. Facebook groups can be easier in English but need more scam checks. A cheaper room is not always cheaper after electricity, commute time, no kitchen, no official mail access, or short lease penalties.

Realistic example: A student finds a NT$8,000 room on 591, but the MRT is a 20-minute bus ride away, electricity is billed separately, and the landlord will not allow official mail. Another NT$10,500 room near campus may be easier for daily life.

Budget

How much does it cost to live in Taipei or New Taipei?

Short answer: Housing drives the budget. Taipei studios can feel expensive, while New Taipei shared rooms may be cheaper but add commute time.

Separate your monthly budget from your arrival budget. The first month often includes deposit, first rent, bedding, cookware, transport cards, document photos, temporary mobile data, and emergency cash.

Realistic example: A student pays NT$11,000 for a shared room in New Taipei, NT$9,000 for food, and NT$1,500 for transport. Her first month still costs much more because she pays two months of deposit plus basic room setup.

Money

How do foreigners open a bank account in Taiwan?

Short answer: Try your employer payroll bank or school-recommended branch first. Bring passport, ARC or resident certificate if available, Taiwan phone number, Chinese address, student ID or employment proof, and extra ID if you have it.

There is no one bank that is always easy for every foreigner. Branch experience matters. Big branches near universities, business districts, science parks, or your employer often understand foreign customers better than a random neighborhood branch. If rejected, ask what document is missing and try again with a stronger file.

Realistic example: A foreign employee goes to the bank her company uses for payroll. HR gives her an employment letter, Chinese office address, and contact person. The account is easier than walking into an unrelated branch with only a passport.

Daily life

How do EasyCard and transportation work in Taiwan?

Short answer: Start with an EasyCard or iPASS. Use MRT and buses in cities, TRA for regular intercity rail, and HSR for faster west-coast trips.

Taiwan is easier once you understand the layers: local transit, intercity trains, high-speed rail, taxis, and bike-sharing. You do not need a scooter immediately, especially in Taipei or New Taipei.

Realistic example: A newcomer living near Banqiao uses EasyCard for MRT, buses, YouBike, and convenience store purchases, then books HSR separately for a weekend trip to Tainan.

Tax

How do foreigners file income tax in Taiwan?

Short answer: Gather your withholding statement, income records, ARC/passport details, and deduction documents, then verify your filing route with eTax or the tax office.

Online filing can be convenient, but not everyone can use every login method. If you cannot use a card reader, Citizen Digital Certificate, or a supported online method, plan time to ask the tax office what alternatives apply.

Realistic example: A foreign teacher has salary income but cannot log in with a card reader on a Mac. He saves his withholding statement, visits the local tax office during filing season, and asks staff which filing method fits his documents.

Reminder: Tax rules, filing tools, and deadlines may change. This guide is educational, not tax advice. Check eTax or a tax office before filing.

Tax

What is the 183-day tax rule in Taiwan?

Short answer: A stay of 183 days or more in a taxable year is a major threshold for Taiwan tax residency, but your income type and documents still matter.

Do not treat 183 days as the only question. Withholding, resident status, income source, filing method, and deductions can all change the final result.

Realistic example: Someone stays in Taiwan for 120 days on a short assignment and receives Taiwan-source salary. Another person stays 210 days as a local employee. Their filing questions and withholding may be very different.

Reminder: Rules may change and individual facts matter. Check Ministry of Finance/eTax guidance or ask the tax office before making decisions.

Work

Do international students need a work permit in Taiwan?

Short answer: Many international students need a valid work permit before paid part-time work. Your school can help, but official labor rules decide.

Do not start paid work based only on what a friend did last year. Ask your school which documents and official system apply to your student category, then wait for approval before your first paid shift.

Realistic example: A student gets a weekend cafe job offer. Before the first shift, she asks the school office how to apply for the student work permit and waits for approval instead of relying on the boss's verbal promise.

Reminder: Labor and work permit rules may change. This is educational information only. Check the Workforce Development Agency, Ministry of Education, and your school before working.

Work

What should I check before signing a job contract?

Short answer: Check job title, salary, pay date, working hours, housing or dorm terms, deductions, probation, overtime, leave, and termination rules.

Ask for a copy you can keep. If the contract language is not clear to you, ask a trusted school office, labor hotline, community group, or qualified advisor before signing.

Realistic example: A foreign worker sees monthly salary written clearly but notices broker fees and dorm charges are only discussed verbally. He asks for the deductions in writing before agreeing.

Reminder: Labor rules and contract rights can depend on the job category. Check Ministry of Labor or local labor office information for your situation.

Work

How should I track salary records and payslips?

Short answer: Keep monthly payslips, bank deposit records, overtime notes, deductions, and screenshots of work schedules in one folder.

Salary records are not only for disputes. They also help with tax filing, renewals, loan or rental applications, and checking whether deductions match what you agreed to.

Realistic example: A factory worker is paid monthly. He saves each payslip photo and bank deposit screenshot, then notices one month has a dorm deduction he did not expect and can ask payroll with evidence.

Reminder: If you suspect unpaid wages or illegal deductions, use official labor help channels or trusted worker support groups. Do not rely only on verbal promises.

Work

What should I know about overtime and leave?

Short answer: Track your work hours, rest days, leave requests, approvals, and overtime payments. Do not rely only on memory.

Different jobs can have different schedules, but your records should be clear enough that you can explain what happened later. Keep screenshots of rosters and messages about schedule changes.

Realistic example: A caregiver writes down start and end times each day and saves messages about holiday work. When pay looks wrong, she has a timeline instead of guessing.

Reminder: Labor rules may change and job categories matter. Confirm with official labor sources or a local labor office before making legal claims.

Safety

What emergency contacts should I save in Taiwan?

Short answer: Save police 110, fire and ambulance 119, anti-fraud 165, protection hotline 113, foreign worker hotline 1955, foreigner hotline 1990, and your address in Chinese and English.

Before there is an emergency, write your address in a note app and keep it visible. Add your nearest hospital, dorm manager, HR contact, school office, and a friend who can translate if needed.

Realistic example: A student has an allergic reaction at night. Her roommate can show the taxi driver the hospital name and Chinese address because they saved it earlier.

Study in Taiwan

How do I ask for help at school?

Short answer: Start with the international office, department office, dorm office, or student advisor. Bring screenshots, dates, and documents.

Schools can be busy, so make your request easy to understand. Ask one clear question at a time and include your student ID, program, deadline, and what you already tried.

Realistic example: A student cannot upload an ARC renewal document. Instead of saying "the system is broken," he emails a screenshot, browser name, deadline, and asks which office can confirm the file.

Reminder: Schools can explain procedures, but immigration, labor, tax, and health insurance decisions still come from official agencies.

Daily life

How do convenience stores work in Taiwan?

Short answer: Convenience stores are for food, parcels, bill payment, ticket machines, printing, ATM access, and everyday small purchases.

They are part of daily infrastructure, not just snack shops. Learn the machine names and ask staff politely if you get stuck, especially with parcel pickup or bill payment slips.

Realistic example: A newcomer orders a desk lamp online and picks it up at FamilyMart with a code. The same week, she pays a utility bill at 7-Eleven and prints a document for school.
Language

What basic Chinese phrases help daily life?

Short answer: Learn phrases for price, address, receipt, no bag, payment, allergies, and asking for help. You do not need perfect Chinese to be functional.

Keep phrases short and show written Chinese when pronunciation fails. Addresses, food allergies, and document names are worth saving in your phone.

Realistic example: Someone with a peanut allergy saves "我對花生過敏" in their phone. At a lunch shop, showing the phrase is faster and safer than trying to explain under pressure.
Newcomer mistakes

What common mistakes do foreigners make in Taiwan?

Short answer: The big mistakes are signing too fast, not keeping records, ignoring official deadlines, budgeting only for normal months, and assuming one friend's case applies to everyone.

Most problems are preventable if you slow down and document things. Save contracts, receipts, messages, screenshots, and official links. When rules are involved, check the source instead of trusting old posts.

Realistic example: An expat signs a one-year lease after one viewing, then learns the commute is 70 minutes and the electricity rate is high. A one-week temporary stay would have given time to compare areas.

Reminder: For tax, immigration, labor, health insurance, and financial services, rules may change. Official sources matter more than social media advice.

Foreign workers

What should foreign workers check first?

Short answer: Prioritize contract clarity, salary records, legal fees, days off, emergency contacts, and safe official channels for help.

English can be useful, but worker-specific content should be built in the languages people actually search in. For Taiwan, that often means Indonesian, Vietnamese, Tagalog, and Thai.

Realistic example: A newly arrived worker keeps photos of the contract, broker fee records, monthly payslips, and the dorm address. If a dispute happens, those records are easier to use than memory.

Reminder: Labor rules may change and can depend on job category. Check official labor sources or trusted worker support channels before acting.
